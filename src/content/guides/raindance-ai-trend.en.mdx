## What the Raindance trend is

The Raindance trend is a short-video edit built around one image: a person standing on a wooden pier above the sea while the sun sits low on the horizon. The clip is warm, slow and a little dramatic, like a frame from a romantic music video. The sound under almost every post is “Raindance” by Dave and Tems.

What makes it a trend rather than just a pretty clip is the cut. Posts usually open on an ordinary photo of someone, at their desk, in a car or in the kitchen, and then cut to that same person on the golden-hour pier. The contrast between normal life and music-video glamour is the punchline. Because the pier clip is generated by AI from a single photo, anyone can take part without travelling to a beach or filming anything.

## Where it came from

The song came first. “Raindance” is a track by British rapper Dave featuring Nigerian singer Tems, released on 23 October 2025 as the fifth track on Dave’s third studio album, The Boy Who Played the Harp. It is a stripped-back Afroswing love song, and the two artists trade lines in a way that feels like a conversation between a couple.

The song became a viral hit between the end of 2025 and early 2026 and climbed to number one on the UK Singles Chart in January 2026. It also reached the top ten in several other countries. The official music video, released on 9 January 2026 and filmed in Lagos, shows the pair as an affectionate couple, including scenes by the sea. That warm, coastal, intimate mood became the visual reference for fan edits.

Once AI video tools could turn one photo into a believable short clip, people started placing themselves into that mood. The golden-hour pier became the shorthand: easy to recognise, flattering for almost anyone and dramatic enough to be funny when the subject is unexpected.

## How a Raindance trend post is built

If you look closely, most posts use the same four ingredients:

1. **The setup photo.** One or two seconds of an everyday picture. Office lighting and messy rooms work best because they make the next shot land harder.
2. **The cut.** A hard cut on the beat to the AI clip.
3. **The pier clip.** The same person on a sunset pier, framed vertically, with slow camera movement and the breeze in their hair.
4. **The sound and caption.** The song from the platform’s library and a short, deadpan caption.

Some creators add a second AI shot, a lyric overlay or a reaction from the person in the photo, but the core never changes.

## The versions people make

The format is flexible, which is why it spread beyond music fans.

- **The completely serious solo.** Your own photo, no joke, just you looking like the lead in a music video.
- **The couple edit.** Two clips, one per person, cut together so they trade the screen the way Dave and Tems trade lines.
- **The family surprise.** A parent or grandparent on the pier, shared with their blessing, often the most-replayed version in group chats.
- **The friend who never sings.** The least dramatic person you know, given the most dramatic shot possible.
- **The work version.** A colleague’s badge photo cut to a sunset pier, posted with their permission for a team channel.

## Rules worth following

A trend built on putting real people into AI video comes with responsibilities. These keep your post fair to everyone involved, including the artists.

- **Consent first.** Only use your own photo or a photo of someone who has agreed. Never use strangers, celebrities or anyone who could be hurt by the post.
- **Children.** A photo of someone under 18 should only be used by their parent or guardian, for ordinary, wholesome family content.
- **Label it.** Use the platform’s AI-generated content label so viewers know the clip is not real footage.
- **Use the official sound.** Add the song from the app’s library instead of ripping audio, and do not cut the official music video into your post.
- **No implied endorsement.** Do not suggest that Dave, Tems or their labels made or approved your clip.

## Is the Raindance trend still worth posting?

Viral sounds peak and fade, and the busiest weeks of this one were around the start of 2026. The format has lasted longer than the peak because the golden-hour pier is a flattering, universal image. It works for anniversaries, birthdays, travel throwbacks and “glow-up” posts with any sound you are allowed to use. If the song is not available on your account, the clip still stands on its own as a sunset moment.

## Summary

The Raindance trend pairs Dave and Tems’ song with a simple cut: an everyday photo, then the same person on a golden-hour pier. You need one good photo, a tool that builds the scene, the song from your app’s library and an AI label. Keep it consensual, keep it labelled and let the sunset do the work.
